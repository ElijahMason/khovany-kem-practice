Station, Standards

Four rules, posted.

These are how Khovany Kem runs a project. They are short because people have to hold them while they work.

  1. One owner per step

    Write one name on the step. That person can ask for help. They cannot hand the step to a group and walk away. If two names are already on it, split the step until each part has one name, or choose. Khovany will make you choose.

  2. A visible queue

    The queue shows open work, the owner, and the next decision. The people who do the work can read it without asking. A message thread is not a queue. A hallway agreement is not a queue. If a line is real, it is posted.

  3. A meeting that ends in a decision

    Open the session against the queue. Close it against the log. The decision is a sentence: what changes, who owns the change, and when the queue will show it. If you cannot write the sentence, you do not have a decision. Write the missing fact and its owner instead, and post that.

  4. A room that matches the process

    The path, the desks, and the hardware follow the map. When a step moves, the thing that step needs moves with it. Khovany keeps this with the project because a correct map in a contrary room trains people to ignore the map.

How to use them

Pick a step that is stuck. Read the four rules against it. You will usually find two owners, a line that is not posted, a meeting that ended in notes, or a walk the room makes longer than the map. Fix that one. Then read the queue again.
