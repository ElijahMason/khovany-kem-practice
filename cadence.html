Station, Cadence

Point at the cadence.

Khovany Kem keeps three objects where the room can read them. The week runs off those objects, not off a slide.

  1. The queue

    Each line is a piece of work, one owner, and the decision that would move it. A line that lives in a side conversation is not in the queue. If you cannot point at it, it is not posted.

  2. The decision log

    A meeting writes into the log before people stand up. The entry names the decision, the owner, and the line on the queue that changes. A recap of what people said is not an entry.

  3. The floor note

    When a desk, a bench, or a piece of hardware blocks a step, it goes on the floor note. Khovany reads that note with the queue. The room stays honest with the map, or the map becomes decoration.

A meeting that ends in a recap did not end.

How a session runs

You open the queue. The owner of the first blocked line says what they need in order to move it. The room decides, or it names the missing fact, the person who will get it, and when that fact will be posted. Then you take the next blocked line. You stop when each blocked line has a decision or a posted next fact.

Khovany will close a session that starts to wander. Wandering feels busy. It leaves the queue where it was.

What he will not add

He will not add a tool to make a fuzzy queue look organized. He recommends a tool when you can name the handoff it removes. If you cannot name the handoff, the tool stays out. Efficiency here is a shorter path from work to owner to decision.
